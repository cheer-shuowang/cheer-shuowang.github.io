01 / About

The short version

I'm a master's student at NUS — you can also call me Allen. I work across human–computer interaction, computational social science, and the digital humanities, using NLP, machine learning, and LLM-based simulation. Most of my work so far has been measuring how people behave in systems; more recently I have been designing systems too, which turns out to be a good way to find out what you did not understand.

I study at the National University of Singapore and work as a research assistant at the CALM Lab. Alongside my studies I interned in strategy and product at Bilibili, Tencent, and Weibo, where I saw at close range how platform design shapes what people do. A lot of my questions started there.

Outside work: travel, the violin, and a fair number of hours in the games I also study.

03 / Research

Three threads

One question runs through all three: how do people — as communities, publics, and cultures — make meaning and act together in a digital age? What differs is where I look and what I do about it. The first designs and evaluates systems, the second measures behaviour at scale, the third turns both on cultural and historical material.

I

Human–Computer Interaction

How people perceive and act inside systems built for them — online communities, games, and the buildings we spend our days in. What a system makes visible, to whom, and what people do about it.
