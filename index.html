01 / About

The short version

I'm a computational social scientist — you can also call me Allen. My work sits where game studies, computational social science, and the digital humanities meet: I use NLP, machine learning, and agent- and LLM-based simulation to understand how digital communities make meaning and act together.

I'm an M.S. candidate at the National University of Singapore, and a research assistant at the CALM Lab. Before academia I interned in strategy and product at Bilibili, Tencent, and Weibo — where I watched, up close, how algorithms and platform design shape what people do. Those years are the field grounding for the questions I now ask.

Outside research: traveling, the violin, and long hours inside the virtual worlds of the games I study.

  • SpineDigital culture & games — identity, discourse, toxicity, and opinion in online communities.
  • MethodComputational social science: NLP & text mining, machine learning, agent-/LLM-based simulation.
  • Second trackDigital humanities & cultural analytics — computation applied to cultural and historical texts.

03 / Research

Three threads

Across games, public life, and cultural media, one question runs through everything: how do people — as communities, publics, and cultures — make meaning and act together in a digital age? A shared computational toolkit ties the threads together.

I

Games, Communities & Identity

How players form communities and contest belonging — how toxicity is perceived differently by different groups, how ACG fandoms polarize around interest-based identities, and how the esports industry turns performance data into a labor market.

II

Public Opinion, Risk & Urban Society

How Chinese publics and cities behave under stress and in everyday life — opinion polarization, crisis and risk governance, and the way urban space shapes social experience. Tools: LDA text mining, LLM-based simulation, social-media analysis, and spatial-temporal modeling.

Digital Humanities & Cultural Analytics

Computational and multimodal-AI methods brought to cultural texts — historical and contemporary alike — building the datasets and models that let us ask new questions of old sources and read cultural change at scale.
